# ProofFlow — Product Vision

*Defined by Spencer, 2026-10-13. This is the product the proofflow.me website,
the free web tool, and the Facebook launch all lead toward.*

## What ProofFlow is

A free proof-of-delivery app & fleet tracker for small delivery teams — and,
with the proof-of-work flow below, a job-proof tool for contractors and
field-service workers (installers, repair techs, cleaners, service calls).

## The proof-of-work flow (the differentiator)

1. **The job arrives as a structured list.** The customer (or dispatcher) sends
   the worker a job whose steps are a clean, well-structured list in the app —
   organized with AI assistance so nothing is vague or missing.
2. **The worker photographs every step.** At the customer's house, as each
   step is completed, the worker takes a picture of it. The result is an
   ordered, timestamped, GPS-stamped photo trail that mirrors the list.
3. **The package goes to the customer.** Sent by email — the customer needs
   nothing installed, just a link. If the customer has the app, everything
   arrives in-app instead.
4. **The customer confirms step by step.** For each photo/step the customer
   taps **Confirm**. Every confirmation notifies the contractor in real time:
   confirmed, approved, liked. The customer watches the job happen without
   standing over it.
5. **The customer signs — and signing is tied to payment.** When all steps are
   confirmed, the customer signs electronically. The signature states the work
   was completed as agreed and is the trigger for payment ("sign to pay").

### Design rules for the loop

- **Non-response must never trap the worker.** Each step is recorded as
  *confirmed*, *seen, unconfirmed*, or *sent, no response* — with timestamps.
  The photo trail itself is the proof; confirmations add strength, and the
  final signature can proceed with the trail visible even if individual steps
  went unconfirmed.
- **The amount is visible before signing.** Sign-to-pay must show exactly
  what is being approved for payment, in plain language, before the
  signature pad appears.
- **Email-first, app-optional for customers.** A one-off customer will not
  install an app. The link does the whole job; the app is the upgrade for
  repeat customers.

## The delivery flow (original product)

Driver gets assigned stops → captures a delivery photo → recipient signs on
the phone → the record is stamped with time + GPS → sender and recipient both
hold the same proof. Disputes ("it never arrived") end with one link.

## Sequencing

- **Now — the free web tool** (proofflow.me/pod-generator): the worker's
  capture half, one device, no accounts — delivery records and step-by-step
  proof-of-work records, printable/saveable, stored only on the device. It is
  genuinely useful today and is what the Facebook campaign advertises.
- **Next — the app**: accounts, structured job lists, the customer
  confirmation loop (email link + in-app), per-confirmation notifications,
  e-signature, and sign-to-pay via a payment provider (Stripe).
- **Monetization today**: Google AdSense on the site's content; the app
  launch converts the waitlist.

## Honesty rules (site and product)

- No claim ships before the feature exists. The website says "relaunching"
  until the app is real; the web tool's records say exactly what they are.
- Customer data (photos, signatures, locations) belongs to the people in the
  record — exportable, deletable, never sold.
